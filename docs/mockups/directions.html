Nexus 4 music player · UI exploration

Three directions for the player

Now Playing and Library, drawn three ways before any Kotlin gets written. Each screen is drawn at 1 CSS pixel = 1 dp, so proportions and type sizes match the real phone. Album names are real examples from a pre-2010 collection; the artwork is abstract placeholder art.

768 × 1280 px · xhdpi (2.0×) 384 × 640 dp status bar 24 dp on-screen nav 48 dp usable 384 × 568 dp

Direction A · lightSleeve

The screen as a record sleeve. Artwork is the only image; the type reads like liner notes, and details a collector cares about (track position, year, bitrate) are shown openly instead of hidden.

On the Nexus 4

  • Cheapest to draw: flat colours, no blur, no per-album colour work.
  • Bundles one serif face for titles; everything else is plain text.
  • A light nav bar needs API 27 (Android 8.1). On 7.1 it stays black, as drawn here.

Trade-off

The most original of the three and the best fit for a curated collection. It may feel calmer and less "app-like" than Apple Music or Spotify.

Direction B · dark · chosenGlow

Artwork-led and dark. Each album tints the whole Now Playing screen with colours taken from its cover, the way the brief's references (Apple Music, Spotify) do it. Big touch targets, a filled play button, a heart for favourites. Chosen, with Sleeve's "Playing on Estudio" output line merged in.

On the Nexus 4

  • Colours come from androidx.palette, computed once per album at scan time and stored. The gradient is a static drawable.
  • No live blur: Android's built-in blur (RenderEffect) needs API 31. A flat gradient gives most of the effect at no cost.
  • The grid needs downscaled thumbnails (about 336 px) and a disk cache. Decoding full-size embedded art would exhaust the heap fast.

Trade-off

The most familiar and "modern" feeling. Also the closest to existing apps, so what makes it distinctive has to come from details.

Direction C · instrumentDeck

The phone as a hi-fi component: a glowing display panel (a VFD, vacuum fluorescent display, like on CD players and amplifiers), physical-looking keys, and a source selector switch that puts the Sonos rooms front and centre.

On the Nexus 4

  • Mostly text and rectangles; the segmented progress bar is a small custom View. Cheap to draw.
  • The output selector makes Sonos a first-class control, which suits the brief's Mode B.
  • Dot-matrix type needs care at small sizes on a 320 dpi screen; keep it for titles and the clock.

Trade-off

The most distinctive, and the one that feels most like a dedicated device. The look may wear thin over time, and long titles need scrolling or truncation on the panel.

Decision

Glow, with Sleeve's "Playing on Estudio · Sonos One SL" line as the output row. Tapping it will open the output picker. Queue, Album detail and the output picker come next, in this direction.

After a pick, the chosen Now Playing screen becomes the first real UI on the device, as part of the Compose vs Views test (roadmap A1).
